# Home By Five, executive brief

**A Macedonia-based growth and search operations company serving US multi-location
businesses, franchise systems and agencies on a per-unit basis.**

Prepared 26 August 2026. Anchor candidates: MaidThis Franchise and Proven Marketing Now.
Live workbench: `index.html`. Network dashboard: `dashboard/index.html`.

Evidence classes are never blended. **FACT** is direct primary evidence. **STRONG
INFERENCE** is several independent signals agreeing. **MODEL** is our own calculation
from stated assumptions. Full citations: `research/source_ledger.csv`.

---

## The short version

The business is viable. The price in the hypothesis is not. Those are two separate
findings, and collapsing them is the fastest way to build a company that cannot pay its
own people.

**$300 per account or location per month is 25 to 42 percent below the modelled cost of
delivering what the brief describes.** The only configuration where it clears cost is a
three-person pod per 40 accounts with no office, no funded expert budget and the owner
drawing $1,500 a month, above roughly 200 accounts. That is a job carrying a company's
risk, not a company.

**$450 works.** At $450 per unit on a MINIMAL pod, a 40-unit book returns 11 percent
gross margin and a 120-unit book returns 26 percent. And $450 is still far below what a
US buyer already spends internally per account, so the savings argument survives
comfortably without going anywhere near $300.

---

## 1. Is this business viable?

Yes, as a priced operating layer. No, as labour arbitrage.

The cost base is real and it is not what "offshore" implies. A senior search strategist
in Skopje costs about **$2,736 a month fully loaded** (MODEL, from FACT-class statutory
rates), not $800. North Macedonia levies 28 percent social contributions on gross salary
plus a 10 percent flat income tax, which means a net salary of MKD 90,000 requires a
gross of MKD 137,304.

The margin therefore has to come from two places: the ratio of units to senior judgement,
and the operating system that lets a junior execute at a senior standard. It cannot come
from paying people badly, because paying people badly produces exactly the quality
failure the buyer is trying to escape.

## 2. Is $300 per location or account viable?

No, not at the described scope.

Modelled breakeven price per unit per month (MODEL, `data/unit_economics.csv`):

| Book size | MINIMAL | LEAN | STANDARD | PREMIUM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40 units | $399 | $457 | $515 | $571 |
| 80 units | $396 | $454 | $512 | $568 |
| 120 units | $333 | $391 | $448 | $504 |
| 200 units | $304 | $362 | $420 | $475 |
| 300 units | $277 | $335 | $393 | $448 |

MINIMAL is three people per 40 accounts: one senior strategist, one junior, one VA. That
is deliberately the same ratio PMNow says it already runs, so it is the honest floor
rather than an aspiration. STANDARD adds a local SEO specialist and an analyst, which is
what the brief's service list actually requires.

The brief describes senior strategy, market analysis, SEO, paid media oversight,
analytics, CRO, funnel analysis, reporting, AI automation, QA, junior execution, vendor
procurement, SOP development, expert knowledge capture and hiring. That is STANDARD at
minimum. STANDARD breaks even at $515 on a 40-unit book.

## 3. At what minimum scale?

**120 units.** Below 80 units the shared company layer dominates: at 40 units the
director's own fully loaded salary is roughly 36 percent of a $12,000 MRR. A single
40-unit pod is a proof of concept, not a business. Two things follow:

- Never sign a first contract below a 40-unit minimum commitment.
- Do not plan the company's cost base around one pod. Plan it around three.

## 4. What gross margin is realistic?

25 to 30 percent at $450 per unit on a MINIMAL pod above 120 units. Reaching a healthy 35
percent on a STANDARD pod needs roughly $690 per unit.

Anyone projecting 60 percent gross margin on this model has either not costed a real QA
layer or is planning to under-deliver. The QA layer is the product.

## 5. What must be excluded from the price?

Non-negotiable exclusions, billed transparently at cost and managed by us without markup:

- All paid media spend
- Premium link and digital PR placements
- External expert consultant fees
- Custom software development
- Call tracking, CRM and third-party licences held in the client's name
- Unusual data costs beyond the standard research stack

If a buyer insists on an all-in number including media, walk away from that structure.
It is the one line that cannot bend, because it makes both the price and the margin
unknowable.

## 6. What are the strongest savings arguments?

Not headcount cost. Three better ones, in order of strength.

**One.** MaidThis is currently hiring a Director of Marketing at **$5,000 to $7,000 a
month** (FACT, job posting updated 10 August 2026) whose posted duties are managing the
Vendor Hub, monitoring paid budgets across franchisee markets, directing SEO vendors,
building per-market KPI dashboards, refreshing the marketing package quarterly and
deploying AI for conversion. That person still needs analysts, specialists and a system
underneath them. We supply the person, the system and the team.

**Two.** Published home-services agency retainers run $1,000 to $3,500 a month for SEO
alone and $2,500 to $12,000 for full service (FACT, agency price guides). A buyer running
50 percent gross margin is already carrying $500 to $1,750 of internal cost per account.
$450 is a saving even before any efficiency is found.

**Three.** Duplicated vendor margin. This one is real but cannot be quantified until
invoices are seen, which is why it must never be promised in advance.

## 7. What are the strongest quality arguments?

**Coverage, measured from the outside.**

Of 46 MaidThis market URL segments found in the search index, only **14 surface a MaidThis
Google Business Profile in the top 20 Google Maps results** for "house cleaning service"
at the city centroid, and only 8 sit in the top 3. Only **1 of 46** reaches the organic
top 20 for "house cleaning services" plus its own city name.

This is a single-point measurement and it has an innocent explanation available: a
suburban territory scanned from a downtown centroid can be genuinely invisible in this
test while trading well. That caveat has to be said out loud. But whatever the
explanation, the fact that an outsider with $13 of API budget can raise 32 open questions
about a 40-plus market network is itself the argument. Nobody is watching every market
every week.

This is also precisely PMNow's stated internal concern, in a different vertical: quality
on accounts nobody is watching.

## 8. What should we guarantee?

Only things fully inside our control. Full tiering in `reports/risk_register.md` and the
Guarantees tab.

- **Coverage cadence.** Every unit receives a documented human review at least every 14
  days. 5 percent fee credit per missed unit-month.
- **Reporting latency.** Monthly reporting by the fifth working day. 2 percent credit per
  late month.
- **Implementation turnaround.** Approved items shipped within 10 working days, clock
  stopped while waiting on client approval. 10 percent credit below 90 percent adherence.
- **QA pass rate.** 95 percent of delivered items pass the published checklist first time.
- **Cost baseline.** A complete, invoice-level fully loaded cost per unit within 60 days,
  or the first 60 days are refunded.

## 9. What should we absolutely not guarantee?

Rankings. Revenue. Lead volume. Any outcome gated by cleaner capacity or franchisee
execution. And above all: **do not promise vendor cost reduction before seeing an
invoice.** The moment it is said it becomes a term of the deal, and every later finding
gets measured against a promise made blind.

Say instead: once the cost baseline exists, part of my compensation can be tied to
reducing controllable fulfilment cost per unit without reducing agreed service levels.

## 10. What would make MaidThis say yes?

A market-by-market picture they do not currently have, handed over before anyone is asked
to pay for it, plus an offer that is cheaper than hiring the Director of Marketing and
separately building the team behind them.

Three things to get right in the room:

- **Do not propose replacing the SEO vendor.** David Lahav founded and runs Lahav Media
  and also owns the MaidThis Denver and Boulder territory, described as the first MaidThis
  franchisee (STRONG INFERENCE, three independent signals). Scorecard delivery, let
  measurement lead.
- **Do not sell more leads.** The founder has said publicly that surveying 40-plus
  franchise owners produced one answer, and it was hiring cleaners, not leads. Sell
  capacity-aware growth.
- **Do not overweight the short-term-rental story.** Airbnb and vacation-rental terms are
  a median of 4 percent of tracked local search demand across the network, 20 percent at
  the very best. The STR wedge is real positioning; it is not where the search volume is.

## 11. What would make PMNow say yes?

One pod, 40 accounts, a fixed cost per account, and a written commitment that no account
goes more than 14 days without a documented human review, with exceptions reported
proactively.

Their problem, stated by their own management, is quality on quiet accounts. On
month-to-month terms with 30 days' notice, a quiet account is a cancellation already
written. Coverage is the product. A ranking guarantee is not.

## 14. Scaling from $10k to $100k MRR

At $450 per unit, STANDARD tier (MODEL):

- **$10k MRR, about 22 units.** One partial pod. The owner does senior delivery. Riskiest
  phase: everything depends on one person staying healthy. Margin is thin to negative;
  treat this as paid product development.
- **$30k MRR, about 67 units.** Two pods. Automation engineer hired, QA lead full time.
  The owner must be out of day-to-day execution here or the ceiling arrives early.
- **$50k MRR, about 111 units.** Three pods, coordinator layer, second senior strategist.
  First point at which the company survives the owner taking two weeks off.
- **$100k MRR, about 222 units.** Second anchor vertical signed. Owner entirely on
  product, pricing and expert acquisition. Single-client concentration must be under 50
  percent by here.

The hard constraint across all four steps is senior hiring, not demand. There are not
many people in Skopje who can own 40 US local-search accounts at a senior standard, and
the second one has to be hired before they are needed, not after.

## 15. What evidence would disprove this business model?

Four things, in order of how much damage each does.

**One.** The anchors' real fully loaded cost per account turns out to be below $400. The
savings argument disappears entirely and the offer has to survive on coverage and QA
alone. This is genuinely possible and is the first question to ask.

**Two.** North Macedonian employer cost is 16 to 18 percent above gross rather than equal
to it. PwC says contributions are withheld from the employee's gross, giving employer cost
equal to gross. Playroll says employers add 16 to 18 percent on top. They cannot both be
right. If Playroll is right, every breakeven in this study rises by roughly that amount and
$450 stops working at 40 units. **A written quote from a Skopje payroll firm settles this
in one phone call and should happen before anything is signed.**

**Three.** The coverage gap is a scanning artefact. Every Maps position here is a single
city-centroid measurement. If a geo-grid scan of three suspect suburban territories finds
MaidThis ranking well off-centroid, the strongest quality argument weakens considerably.
Re-measure before presenting the finding, and present it as a question either way.

**Four.** Neither anchor will separate media and hard costs from the fee. That makes the
price meaningless, the margin unknowable and the guarantee unenforceable.

## What this study does not contain

Stated plainly, because a feasibility study that hides its gaps is marketing.

- **The current MaidThis FDD.** Everything about the Digital Marketing Package, the local
  marketing requirement and the 2025 or 2026 Item 19 and Item 20 tables rests on the
  client's own reading of the 2024 document. Two headline figures from that reading were
  independently corroborated (the FY2023 net loss above $109,000 and the Item 20 count of
  13 franchised outlets at end-2023). **The Digital Marketing Package line was not.** No
  public FDD summary reachable in this run discloses it. Buying the current FDD is the
  cheapest risk reduction available and must happen before any marketing-pool number is
  quoted to anyone.
- **Demographic and short-term-rental overlays.** The US Census ACS API now requires a
  key, so population, household counts, median income and tenure split were not collected.
- **Geo-grid Maps scanning.** Single-centroid only, as noted above.
- **The 100-company lookalike list.** Specified in `reports/market_opportunity.md`, not
  built. Assembling 100 names from directory scraping would look like research and behave
  like noise, and DataForSEO Business Listings bills per result, so it needs its own
  budget decision.
- **Any PMNow-internal number.** Client count, ARPA, tenure, churn and consultant spend
  are unknowable from outside. Third-party estimates span a tenfold range and none should
  be repeated as fact, including the $1.63M figure in circulation.

**Recommended first anchor.**

## Commercial terms

| | |
|---|---|
| Unit | One active client account |
| Brief's hypothesis | $300 per account per month |
| Modelled breakeven at 40 accounts, MINIMAL tier | $399 |
| Modelled breakeven at 120 accounts, MINIMAL tier | $333 |
| **Recommended opening price** | **$450 per active account per month** |
| Minimum commitment | 40 accounts billed |
| MRR at 40 accounts | $18,000, 11.3% gross margin |
| MRR at 120 accounts | $54,000, 26.1% gross margin |
| Path to $300 | Only above 200 accounts, on a stripped pod, with the expert budget billed separately |
| Term | 6 months minimum, then 60-day mutual notice |

The 6-month minimum matters more here than anywhere else in this study. PMNow sells
month-to-month with 30 days' notice. Accepting the same terms on the supply side would
mean building a company whose survival horizon is 30 days.

## In scope

- Senior diagnosis on onboarding and on a fixed review cadence
- Local search strategy, GBP and Maps programme, citations and entity work
- Technical QA and implementation against a published checklist
- Content planning and internal linking
- Reporting and account health monitoring with proactive escalation
- Consultant findings translated into SOPs, tested on a cohort, then rolled across the book
- Portfolio QA: every account reviewed by a human on a committed cadence

## Excluded, billed transparently at cost

Media spend, premium placements, external expert fees, custom development, third-party
licences held in PMNow's name.

## The line that sells this offer

> "Your risk is not that the work is bad. Everything public says the work is good: 4.8
> from a hundred-plus reviews and no complaint pattern anywhere.
>
> Your risk is that on a month-to-month book, an account can go quiet for eight weeks
> before anyone notices, and by then the cancellation is already written. The accounts
> that churn are never the loud ones.
>
> I will guarantee that no account in my pod goes more than fourteen days without a
> documented human review, and I will send you the exceptions myself before you have to
> ask."

That is a coverage guarantee. It is enforceable, it is entirely inside our control, and it
is aimed precisely at the problem management has already named out loud.

## The four numbers to ask for

Ask in the first conversation. All four are single questions, and the answers determine
whether the offer is about savings or about coverage.

1. **Fully loaded fulfilment cost per account per month.** If it is above $500 the savings
   argument is strong. If it is below $400 there is no savings argument and the offer runs
   on coverage and QA alone.
2. **Average client monthly retainer.** Determines what fraction of their revenue our fee
   represents and therefore how much resistance the price meets.
3. **Average client tenure and monthly churn.** Determines what a coverage guarantee is
   worth in dollars. If churn is 3 percent a month on a $1,800 ARPA book of 110 accounts,
   a single percentage point of churn avoided is roughly $24,000 a year of retained
   revenue. That is the arithmetic that makes the fee look small.
4. **Annual outside consultant and vendor spend.** Determines whether the expert-capture
   system is a headline feature or a footnote.

## The expert-capture pitch

The founder has adopted Jason Hennessey's outsourcing-genius method, which is documented
publicly. Do not offer to replace the experts: the expert bench is part of the product and
part of the founder's identity.

Offer to multiply them:

> "The value is not only getting the expert into the room. It is how fast what they say
> becomes part of the product across a hundred accounts.
>
> Right now each engagement produces a call, some notes and a Slack thread, and most of it
> evaporates. I would record it, extract each recommendation as a testable claim, run it on
> a matched cohort with a holdout, measure whether the predicted metric actually moved,
> turn what worked into an SOP and a QA rule, and then roll it across the book.
>
> The next expert you hire starts by reading what the previous five recommended, what was
> tested, what worked, and under which conditions it failed. The fourth expert should cost
> you less to absorb than the first one did. Right now it costs exactly the same."

**Do not quote the budget figures.** The five-to-ten-thousand-a-month and five-thousand-
per-audit numbers in circulation could not be verified. Referencing the method is safe;
inventing the amounts back at the person who actually pays them is not.

## What to expect as objections

**"Why would I pay $450 when I can hire a VA for $800 a month?"**
Because a VA is not a strategist, and the problem is not execution capacity, it is senior
attention on accounts nobody is watching. A pod is one senior strategist, one junior and
one VA, plus a QA layer, plus the person who replaces any of them within two weeks.

**"My internal cost is lower than that."**
Then the offer is coverage and QA, not savings, and the guarantee tier carries it. Ask for
the number before pricing rather than after.

**"What happens if your strategist quits?"**
That is the point of the model. Turnover is on our side of the line, it is priced into the
contingency, and the SLA does not change.

**"I want to start with ten accounts."**
Ten accounts does not fund a pod and produces exactly the thin coverage the offer exists to
fix. Forty is the floor. If they will not commit to forty, a paid 60-day diagnostic on
their full book is a better first step than a bad first contract.

## First 90 days

| Window | Objective | Deliverable | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days 1 to 30 | Baseline | Fully loaded cost per account established, account health scored across the pod's 40, coverage log live | Cost number agreed in writing |
| Days 31 to 60 | Prove coverage | Every account reviewed on the 14-day cadence, weekly exception report, first consultant finding put through the test-to-SOP pipeline | Coverage adherence above 95 percent |
| Days 61 to 90 | Institutionalise | SOP library live, QA checklist published, account health dashboard handed over, pricing agreed for the ongoing term | A signed number, and a decision on pod two |

## Why this is the better first anchor

The unit is already defined. The delivery ratio is already known. There is one decision
maker. Forty near-identical HVAC and plumbing accounts are far more repeatable than 46
heterogeneous franchise territories with 46 different owners. And the buyer has already
said the problem out loud, which removes the hardest part of any sale.

MaidThis is the better second customer and a considerably better case study.

# Business model

---

## 1. What is being sold

A **dedicated growth and search operations unit**, priced per active account or location,
operated from North Macedonia for US multi-location businesses, franchise systems and
agencies.

The buyer keeps brand, customer and franchisee relationships, media ownership, strategic
approval and commercial ownership. We own staffing, payroll, workspace, internal
management, QA, delivery capacity, training, workflows, automation, approved
subcontractors and day-to-day delivery.

The buyer is not renting people. They are buying **capacity with an accountable owner**,
and the accountable owner is one person rather than a vendor list.

## 2. Why this is not outsourcing

Outsourcing sells hours at a lower rate. This sells a unit of covered account at a fixed
price, with the quality bar, the QA layer and the replacement risk carried on our side.

The distinction is commercially load-bearing:

| Outsourcing | This model |
|---|---|
| Client specifies the work | We specify the work, client approves the strategy |
| Client absorbs turnover | We absorb turnover |
| Client manages the individuals | Client manages one relationship |
| Priced per hour or per head | Priced per unit covered |
| Quality is the client's problem | Quality is a contractual commitment |
| Scales by adding headcount the client sees | Scales behind one unchanged interface |

The moment the price is quoted per person, the model collapses back into outsourcing and
the buyer starts comparing hourly rates with Manila. Price per unit, always.

## 3. Positioning language, and what to avoid

**Never say cheap labour, offshore, or nearshore.** These reframe the conversation as a
rate comparison and invite a race the model cannot win.

**Say:** European cost base, senior US-market judgement, one accountable operator, an
AI-native operating system, and capacity that scales without the buyer hiring anyone.

The buyer's mental arithmetic should end at "my current fulfilment cost per account is X;
this is X minus something, with better visibility and a written SLA". How our margin works
is our business, not theirs.

## 4. The unit

One **account** (agency model) or one **location or territory** (franchise model). The
unit is what makes this repeatable across verticals:

| Vertical | Unit |
|---|---|
| Local SEO agency | Client account |
| Franchise system | Territory |
| Multi-location home services | Branch |
| Dental or med spa group | Practice |
| Legal network | Market |

## 5. Delivery architecture

**Pod layer.** One delivery team per 40 units. Composition varies by service tier.
Scales linearly with the book.

**Company layer.** Director, QA lead, automation engineer, paid media, coordination.
Shared across pods, grows in steps. This is the only source of operating leverage in the
model, and it is also the reason small books lose money.

**Expert layer.** A funded budget for outside specialists, whose findings are captured,
tested, converted into SOPs and QA rules, and rolled across the portfolio. This is the
part that compounds. Detail in `reports/pmnow_deep_dive.md` section 6.

## 6. Where the margin actually comes from

Three places, in order of importance.

**One, the ratio of units to senior judgement.** A senior strategist covering 40 units
instead of 15 is the entire game. Everything in the operating system exists to raise that
ratio without lowering the standard.

**Two, the operating system.** Standardised diagnosis, scored markets, automated
monitoring and a QA checklist mean a junior executes at a senior standard on the
repeatable 70 percent of the work. The senior person spends their time on the 30 percent
that actually needs them.

**Three, the cost base.** Real, but the smallest of the three and the least defensible.
Anybody can hire in a lower-cost country. Nobody can copy a knowledge base of tested
recommendations across 200 accounts in a weekend.

If the pitch leans on the third, the buyer will eventually find someone cheaper. If it
leans on the first two, they will not.

## 7. What must never be inside the price

- All paid media spend
- Premium link and digital PR placements
- External expert consultant fees
- Custom software development
- Call tracking, CRM and third-party licences held in the client's name
- Unusual third-party data costs

We manage these and never mark them up. The transparency is the point: it is what makes
the per-unit price legible and the savings argument checkable.

A buyer who insists on an all-in number including media is asking us to absorb a variable
we do not control. Decline that structure.

## 8. Scaling path

At $450 per unit, STANDARD tier (MODEL, `data/unit_economics.csv`):

| Stage | Units | People | What changes | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $10k MRR | ~22 | 4 to 5 | Owner does senior delivery | Total key-person dependency |
| $30k MRR | ~67 | 9 to 11 | Automation engineer, full-time QA | Owner still in delivery |
| $50k MRR | ~111 | 15 to 18 | Third pod, coordinator, second senior strategist | Senior hiring pipeline |
| $100k MRR | ~222 | 30 plus | Second anchor vertical, owner on product only | Client concentration |

The binding constraint at every stage is **senior hiring, not demand**. There are not many
people in Skopje who can own 40 US local-search accounts at a senior standard. The second
one must be hired before they are needed, and the price has to fund that.

## 9. What kills this business

In order of likelihood:

1. **Signing at $300.** Structural, permanent, and the first number said out loud tends to
   become the ceiling.
2. **Single-client concentration.** One 30-day cancellation clause ends the company. Do
   not build past two pods for one client without a second anchor signed.
3. **The owner staying in delivery past $30k MRR.** The ceiling arrives quietly and by
   then the cost base assumes a director who is actually doing analyst work.
4. **All-in pricing that swallows media.** Makes margin unknowable and guarantees
   unenforceable.
5. **Hiring for cost rather than judgement.** The product is quality on accounts nobody is
   watching. A pod staffed to a budget cannot deliver it, and the buyer notices in month
   four.

## 10. What this business is genuinely good at

Worth stating, because the rest of this document is deliberately adversarial.

The combination that is rare: someone who has run US local-search delivery at scale,
operating a team in a European timezone with a real cost advantage, building the
measurement layer themselves rather than buying it, and willing to sign a coverage SLA
that most agencies would not.

That combination is not common, and the two anchor conversations exist because of it. The
job of this study is to make sure it gets priced like it is.

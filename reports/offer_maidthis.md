**Reference point for the buyer:** they are currently hiring one Director of Marketing at
$5,000 to $7,000 a month, and that person still needs analysts, specialists and a system
underneath them.

## In scope

- Per-market scorecard: demand, visibility, review equity, competitive position, funnel
- Vendor scorecarding and monthly performance review against measured outcomes
- Local search and Maps programme management across all active territories
- Paid media oversight and budget allocation recommendations; media itself excluded
- Landing page and conversion work on franchise location pages
- Reporting, anomaly detection and quarterly market plans
- Capacity-aware acquisition planning once cleaner-supply data is available
- The pod: strategy, local SEO, analysis, junior execution, QA, management, tooling,
  workspace, hiring and replacement

## Explicitly excluded, billed transparently at cost

- All paid media spend
- Premium link and digital PR placements
- External expert consultant fees
- Custom software development
- Call tracking, CRM and third-party licences held in MaidThis's name
- Unusual data costs beyond the standard research stack

We manage all of the above and never mark any of it up.

## What we can show before being paid anything

The `dashboard/` in this repository, populated with $13 of public data:

- 46 market pages tracked, with search demand, weighted CPC and modelled cost per lead
- 14 markets where a MaidThis profile appears in the Maps top 20, 8 in the top 3
- 32 markets where no MaidThis profile appears at the centroid
- 1 of 46 markets ranking in the organic top 20 for its own city term
- Full Maps pack composition per market, with competitor review counts
- A transparent opportunity score with every component published

This is the offer's strongest asset and it costs nothing to hand over.

## The three things to get right in the room

**One. Do not propose replacing the SEO vendor.** David Lahav founded and runs Lahav Media
and also owns the MaidThis Denver and Boulder territory, described as the first MaidThis
franchisee. Three independent signals agree. Scorecard delivery, let measurement lead.

**Two. Do not sell more leads.** The founder has said publicly that surveying 40-plus
franchise owners produced one answer: hiring cleaners, not leads. An offer promising more
leads answers a question nobody asked, and worse, it produces exactly the incomplete-clean
and scheduling complaints already visible in the review set.

**Three. Do not overweight the short-term-rental story.** Airbnb and vacation-rental terms
are a median of 4 percent of tracked local search demand across the network, 20 percent at
the very best. The STR wedge is real positioning and a real customer segment. It is not
where local search volume lives.

## The framing that works

Not "I will do your SEO." Not "I am cheaper than that hire."

> "You are about to hire one person to sit between several vendors, forty-something
> markets, a network of franchisees and a marketing package that needs refreshing every
> quarter. That person will be good. They will also spend their first year building the
> team and the system that should already exist underneath them.
>
> I can bring the person, the system and the team as one thing. You keep the brand, the
> franchisee relationships, the budgets and every strategic decision. I carry the
> staffing, the payroll, the QA and the replacement risk.
>
> Before I claim it saves you money, I want thirty days with the vendor invoices and the
> funnel data. Then I will tell you what each market actually costs you to service and
> what the target should be. If I cannot find enough there to justify the model, you
> should not continue with it."

## The honest weakness

Forty-six territories with forty-six owners is forty-six relationships, not one. Franchisee
cooperation is not in our control and it is not in the franchisor's control either.

Price per territory, but scope the work at the corporate layer. Either price owner-level
coordination separately or exclude it explicitly in writing. Otherwise the pod drowns in
owner-by-owner coordination that no financial model accounts for, and the margin
disappears without anyone being able to point at where it went.

## Longer-term, deliberately not in the first conversation

A provider allocation and scoring network, matching bookings to qualified local cleaners
by availability, response time, acceptance rate, completion rate, rating and distance, is
a genuinely strong idea and directly addresses the stated capacity bottleneck.

It is also a large software build that would take twelve months, sits outside a per-unit
operating fee, and would raise an obvious question about who owns the resulting technology.
Raise it as a direction of travel in month six, not as part of the opening offer. Promising
nineteen products before Christmas is how a credible operator starts sounding like a
vendor.
